### 1. Purpose

Jordan raised two questions with me last week about returns on creator-code orders. Looking into them led me back to the Pulse Creator Collective agreement of 15 December 2022 (the "Agreement"), which comes up for renewal in January. This is not a full review of our consumer terms.

### 2. The 60-day window is a contractual obligation

Under Schedule B, Section 2.5 of the Agreement, Zenith must offer a 60-day return window on all orders placed with a creator code and must state it at checkout. Our standard window is 30 days. The longer window is not a goodwill policy that marketing or CX can shorten on its own, and it survives termination for orders placed before the codes are deactivated (Section 8.3).

The obligation runs in one direction only. The Agreement requires us to offer 60 days. It does not require us to accept returns after 60 days.

### 3. Returns are being approved after the window

CX tells me that agents regularly see creator-code returns approved by the return form well after day 60. Jordan's team gave me several examples from September and October, a number of them past day 70. As far as CX can tell, the system **does not seem to enforce the window** on creator orders. Standard orders are rejected at day 31 as expected.

I am not the right person to investigate how the return form works. Someone who owns the storefront services should confirm what it actually checks.

### 4. Why this matters: refunds and disputes

- When we refund an old order, the customer's card statement shows a credit months after the purchase. Customers who have waited weeks for a refund often open a dispute with their bank before it arrives ("credit not processed"). If the dispute goes against us, Meridian Payments, our card processor, charges a dispute fee on top, and we can end up refunding twice.
- Card networks monitor each merchant's **dispute ratio**. Above a threshold, generally set just under 1% of transactions, a merchant is placed in a monitoring program with fines and higher processing rates, and in the worst case loses card acceptance. I don't have our current ratio. Priya, could finance ask Meridian for it?

### 5. Schedule B, Section 2.3: creator codes are not combinable

The Agreement also provides that creator codes "are not combinable with any other promotion, discount or code," including sitewide sales. Section 7.2 obliges us to configure checkout so that codes are applied in accordance with Schedule B.

The cart currently displays the banner **"GOT A CREATOR CODE? STACK IT WITH OUR SITEWIDE SALE!"** On its face, this invites customers to do what the Agreement says they cannot. I don't know whether checkout actually lets the two combine. Either way, the banner and the contract say different things, and that inconsistency should be resolved. Megan, I'd like to understand the history of the banner and whether Pulse has ever raised it.

### 6. Recommendations

1. **Before the renewal decision** (written notice is due by **17 January 2024**), Legal, Marketing, CX and Finance should review together how creator orders are handled in practice: the return window, discount combination and dispute exposure.
2. Ask the owner of the return form to confirm that the 60-day limit is enforced. Until then, CX should check the order date manually before approving any creator-code return.
3. Review the "stack it" banner against Schedule B, Section 2.3 with Marketing.
4. Finance to obtain the current dispute ratio from Meridian Payments.
