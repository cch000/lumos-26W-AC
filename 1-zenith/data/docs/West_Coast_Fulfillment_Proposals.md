Priya,

You asked me after the November sync to look at whether a West Coast 3PL would get our shipping cost down. Two vendors have quoted and the summaries are below. I haven't recommended either one. The commercial terms need your team's eyes, and I'd want the payback worked out properly before anyone signs a 24- or 36-month contract.

## Background

- Everything ships from Edison. West Coast orders cost us about **$15 per order** in carrier charges this year, compared with roughly $6 to $8 for East, North and South.
- We're on pace for about **7,500 West orders** in 2023, around 20% of volume.
- West wasn't always this far out of line. For most of 2022 it ran just under $10 an order.
- Most West parcels on the NorthStar invoices are billed as **Express Air**. Nobody on my team selects express. The labels come out of the printer that way.

## Quote 1: WestBridge Fulfillment (Reno, NV)

| Item | Quote |
|---|---|
| Setup, systems integration and initial inventory transfer | ~$65,000 one-time (transfer freight estimated; billed at cost) |
| Pick and pack | $3.10 per order, plus $0.35 per additional unit |
| Storage | $21 per pallet per month |
| Receiving | $38 per pallet |
| Outbound | "2-day ground to every West ZIP for $5–6 per parcel" on their carrier contracts |
| Term | 24 months, 90 days' notice |

Notes from the call (rep: their regional sales lead):
- They'd hold a forward stock of our top sellers and we'd replenish from Edison by truckload every two to three weeks. Slow movers stay with us.
- They put a lot of weight on their floor process. Their pickers use **batch and zone picking** ("one picker, one cart, twelve orders"), and they say it cuts picker walking by **40–50% compared with single-order picking**. That's how they absorb peaks without overtime. It was part of the sales pitch, but it was the most interesting thing I heard all week.
- They want a 6-week onboarding window, so a February signature means April go-live at the earliest.

## Quote 2: Pacific Parcel Partners (Ontario, CA)

| Item | Quote |
|---|---|
| Setup, integration and inventory transfer | ~$80,000 one-time |
| Fulfillment fee | $2.80 per order, all-in up to 3 units |
| Storage | included up to 40 pallets; $24 per pallet per month above |
| Minimum monthly fee | $7,500 (fulfillment and storage count toward it) |
| Outbound | "2-day ground to all West ZIPs, $5–6 per parcel" |
| Term | 36 months; early exit fee of 3 months' minimums |

Notes:
- Closer to the LA population, which is where most of our West orders go.
- The minimum monthly fee applies from month one, whatever our volume.
- Their Shopify connector is "standard", but they'd need someone on our side to map our shipping and notification setup. I told them I'd have to check who that is.

## Things neither quote covers

- **Split inventory.** We'd carry two stocks of the core range. That means more inventory and more cash tied up, and it means two places where a SKU can run out.
- **Returns.** Both would process West returns locally for an extra fee (not quoted yet). Otherwise returns keep coming back to Edison.
- **Our own costs don't go away.** Edison rent and staff stay the same. The 3PL fees come on top for the West volume.

Zone 8 is the far West from here: Arizona, Nevada, California and the Pacific Northwest. Ground transit to zone 8 is 5 to 6 business days, and the 3PLs are selling against that.

## My two cents

I can see why the 3PLs are attractive on paper. But I keep coming back to the rate card. Ground to zone 8 is **$9.40**, so even our worst ground lane is well under what we're paying per West order. I don't fully understand why our West invoices look like express. I've asked NorthStar for a lane-level breakdown of the last three months and haven't had it back yet. I'd like to understand that before we sign anything long-term.

Happy to set up calls with either vendor if you want to dig in.

Dana
