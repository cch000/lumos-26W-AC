# The Challenge: The OmniMart Crisis

Pick one of OmniMart's problems, come up with a digital solution, and **build a working demo of it** today. Then convince us it's worth it for OmniMart.

## The Situation
**OmniMart** is a big supermarket chain. This case is about its **Vienna region** (about 40 stores). It sells everything from fresh groceries to electronics and basic clothing, and it also runs *in-store cafés, click-and-collect and a home-delivery fleet*. Its loyalty app (with the online shop inside) has about **180,000 monthly users**.

OmniMart's regional manager ran a 6-month audit. It found inefficiencies that cost the region **millions of euros** and damage OmniMart's sustainability reputation. The audit shows three crisis areas. The manager already has an idea for each one.

> **The audit's main figures** (rounded, Vienna region, 6 months). Use them, or replace them with your own assumptions and say so. 
> - About **1,200 t of food binned**, most of it bakery and fresh produce at closing time. Roughly **€2.5M** in written-off goods.
> - Home delivery costs about **€9 per order** but customers pay **€3.90**. Vans leave **about half full** on average.
> - Plant-based products make up **about 5 %** of meat and protein sales but **about 20 %** of that category's write-offs. Their margin is roughly **twice** that of meat.

## The 3 Problems

### Problem 1: The Waste Crisis
* **The problem:** Stores throw away a lot of unsold perishable food (mainly bread and produce) at closing time. Delivery customers bulk-buy perishables and complain that they go off at home.
* **The manager's idea:** an app feature that tracks the expiry dates of what a customer bought and suggests recipes to use it up in time.
* **A demo could be:** a screen or script that takes a shopping list with dates and shows "use these first" plus a matching recipe. Or a staff tool that shows what to discount before closing. Or a customer "rescue bag" offer in the app.

### Problem 2: The Logistics Bleed
* **The problem:** Delivery loses money on every order. Routes are inefficient and vans leave half-empty.
* **The manager's idea:** neighbourhood drop zones. Customers pick a community locker or a partner shop instead of door-to-door delivery.
* **A demo could be:** a checkout step where the customer picks a drop point on a map and sees the price difference. Or a small planner that groups orders into fuller routes. Or the data model behind lockers, slots and pick-ups.

### Problem 3: The Eco-Friendly Slump
* **The problem:** Customers mostly buy meat. Higher-margin plant-based alternatives are overlooked, expire on the shelf and get thrown away.
* **The manager's idea:** a cart recommendation that suggests a plant-based swap when a customer adds meat.
* **A demo could be:** a mock cart where adding certain products triggers a swap suggestion with your message, and the cart updates when the customer accepts. Or a dashboard that flags slow-moving plant-based stock for promotion.

**The manager's ideas are a starting point, not an order.** Each one has weak spots: who actually uses it, whether it pays, what it changes in the stores. If you see a better solution, build that instead.

## Your Mission
You are a cross-functional team (tech, business, marketing, design). Fix **one** of these problems by building **one product together**.

You decide:

- how you show that it works;
- how OmniMart gets customers or staff to actually use it, and what it earns or saves. Including a marketing and business insight.

Any stack is fine, including no-code tools, Figma click-throughs, spreadsheets and AI tools. Use what gets you to a working demo fastest.

## What You Deliver

2. **A working demo** of **one complete flow** (for example: customer adds meat → sees the swap → accepts → cart updates). It may use fake data and hard-coded parts. Add 2–3 sentences on how you built it and what a real version would need (data, systems, integration with the app).

## Ground Rules
- There is no single correct solution. A simple idea that is well argued beats a complicated one.
- You don't have to build a whole platform. One flow that works is enough.
- Question the manager's assumptions and the numbers above. If you change a number, say why. Those assumptions are not necessary correct.
- Work as one team on one product. Teams that split into "tech" and "slides" and merge at the end usually show it.
- AI tools are allowed. Any other tools are also allowed. You are responsible for every number and claim, and we may ask how you checked it.

## Final Presentation
**5–7 minutes** of pitch, followed by **3–5 minutes** of Q&A, where we may ask anyone about any part.

**Remember:** we're not looking for a finished, bug-free app. We're looking for *thoughtful* problem-solving, a clear technical idea, a realistic business argument, real teamwork, and proof that you can turn an idea into something tangible.
