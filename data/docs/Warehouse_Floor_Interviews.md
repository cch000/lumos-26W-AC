# Warehouse floor conversations: notes

**Author:** Priya Raman (CFO)
**Date:** Tuesday, 28 November 2023, 1:30–3:00 PM, Edison warehouse
**People:** Luis M. (picker), Tasha R. (packer), R. Gupta (shift supervisor)
**Format:** Three informal 15-minute conversations on the floor during the afternoon wave. Handwritten notes, typed up the same evening. Quotes are as close to verbatim as I could get.

*Context for myself: overtime and picking errors both keep climbing on the ops dashboard. I wanted to hear how the work actually runs before the January board. Harold knew I was coming and was fine with it. He was in a carrier meeting for most of the afternoon.*

---

## 1. Luis M., picker (with us since March)

Luis picks from a paper list. One order, one list, one trip. He walks the loop, pulls the items into a tote and brings the tote to the pack bench, then picks up the next list.

- "Every order is the whole loop. Front to back and back again. If someone orders socks, I walk the whole building for socks."
- I asked how far that is. He said Harold's SOP has it at about 300 feet a trip. "Feels longer by Saturday."
- Heavy items are the worst. The Hex Dumbbell Pair 10kg Bundle and the kettlebells are at the back. "You do three of those in a row, your back knows."
- Eco items don't go to the normal benches. If an order has anything from Terra, or the Eco Hydration Bottle, he takes it to the Green Station at the far end. "That's another walk, maybe the same again."
- On mistakes: "The black socks and the navy socks are next to each other. At 7 PM, under these lights, they're the same sock." Same for the Velocity Training Shorts in Black and Navy. He says most of his mistakes come at the end of a long shift.
- Weekends: "Saturdays have been mandatory since spring. Some Sundays too. They pay overtime, I'm not complaining about the money. I'm complaining about the Saturdays."

## 2. Tasha R., packer

Tasha works the main pack bench and covers the Green Station when it's busy.

- Standard box: three strips of tape, the insert card and the returns slip. "Every box, every time. Even a single pair of socks."
- Eco orders get hand-wrapped in the compostable wrap and tied with twine, and that can only be done at the Green Station because that's where the wrap is kept. "It's beautiful. Customers post it. It's also slow."
- **The Green Station lights have been out since August.** "Half the strip is dead and the other half flickers. Facilities put in a ticket, they said parts are on backorder. We've got a work lamp clamped to the shelf." Eco orders pile up there in the afternoons. She showed me two carts of totes waiting.
- On the stamp: any order with more than three units can't be packed until a supervisor signs off on the pick list with the stamp. "If the supervisor is on the dock or on a call, those totes just sit. I pack what I can and the stamped ones wait."
- Unprompted: "Since spring we get CX coming down here every day asking us to look up tracking numbers. Customers say they never got the shipping e-mail. That's not us. We print the label, it goes on the box, the system sends the e-mail. At least it used to. Now they just call."

## 3. R. Gupta, shift supervisor

Gupta has been a shift supervisor since 2021, one of the longest-serving people on the floor.

- He described his day as "stamping and firefighting". The stamp rule (supervisor check on orders over three units, SOP 4.1) is "Harold's rule, and it's a good rule. Big orders are where the expensive mistakes are." But it means he is tied to the stamp desk. When he steps away, the queue builds.
- On errors: "We were very clean when we were three people and a thousand orders a month. Now we are tired people and four thousand orders. The new people learn on the job, on overtime."
- **The batch-picking pilot.** I hadn't heard about this before. In September 2022 Harold let them try batch picking for two weeks: one picker took several orders at once on a cart with dividers, and the orders were sorted at the bench. "Honestly, on the floor, it felt faster. Less walking, for sure. The pickers liked it." It stopped after two weeks. "Batches come in all at once, so ten orders with four units land on the stamp desk at the same moment. The desk was full of totes. Harold said it was chaos and we went back to one order at a time."
- I asked whether he'd try it again. Long pause. "With the stamp the way it is? I don't know. It's not my decision."
- On the Green Station: "Everyone walks eco items to one corner, and that corner has no light. I've asked twice."
- On weekends: he has been scheduling mandatory Saturday shifts since spring and says two of the March hires are "close to quitting". Temps help in peaks but need supervision, "which is me, at the stamp desk."
